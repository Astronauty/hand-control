% \section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our work focuses on teleoperated pick-and-place tasks, providing wrench-feasibility
guarantees for a human operator working with vision-based hand tracking. We
demonstrate this in a simulated pick-and-place scene in
MuJoCo~\cite{todorovMuJoCoPhysicsEngine2012}. We assume that for every object
$o_k \in \mathcal{O}$, we have its geometry and pose, its coefficient of friction
$\mu_k$, mass and inertia, which we use to guarantee collision-free trajectories and
wrench feasibility.

Our method allows an operator to drive the task through four phases, illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:controller_overview}. The \texttt{Approach} phase tracks the retargeted
human hand pose while recommending a certified grasp on the nearest object. The
\texttt{Pick} phase removes the human from the loop and plans to the committed
configuration. The \texttt{Transport} phase returns wrist control to the operator
under a slew limit that keeps the object wrench inside the certified set, and the
operator releases the object at the \texttt{Place} location.

To recommend feasible grasps to the user prior to \texttt{Pick}, we formulate grasp
synthesis as a single nonlinear program that couples kinematic reachability, contact
placement on the object surface, and wrench feasibility, and we solve it by a sequence
of relinearized subproblems. The resulting contact set is then executed by an
internal-force controller whose commanded squeeze is certified against an explicit
disturbance budget, and whose motion is rate-limited so that the wrench feasibility
budget is satisfied during live teleop.

\subsection{Contact seeding and candidate selection}
\label{sec:seeding}

Object geometry is taken from the YCB object set~\cite{calli2015ycb}, distributed as
triangulated scans. The scans are open surfaces and are not directly usable by either
the simulator or the optimizer, so each object is preprocessed once: sealed to a
watertight solid by voxelisation, decomposed into convex collision hulls, and assigned
an inertia tensor scaled to the published YCB mass. The explicit inertial is required
because MuJoCo otherwise infers mass from the visual mesh together with every
overlapping hull, giving \SI{0.379}{\kilo\gram} for a \SI{0.066}{\kilo\gram} object.

The planner does not act on the mesh directly. A triangulated surface is $C^0$: its
normal is piecewise constant and jumps across every edge, so a mesh-constrained contact
has a discontinuous Jacobian and requires three variables and a surface equality.
Instead, a quadratic patch (Sec.~\ref{sec:contact-param}) is fitted around each seed to
the dense visual mesh, which carries \num{8194} vertices on a representative object
against \num{466} for its collision decomposition and is the only one of the two fine
enough to support a local least-squares fit. Each contact then reduces to an
unconstrained $\bm{t}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ that remains on the surface by construction
and carries the differentiable normal \eqref{eq:sym-normal}.

Because the patch is valid only over the trust region \eqref{eq:trust}, the seed
determines both the initial iterate and the region of surface the solver can reach.
Candidates are therefore over-generated and screened before the solve. One seed is
placed deterministically along the object's minor principal axis through the hull
centroid; the remainder march along randomized directions. Each march retains the
first and last zero crossings of $\phi$ along the ray, which places both contacts on
the outer surface: the nearest crossing is correct only for solid bodies, since the
interior void of a container registers as exterior ($\phi > 0$) and projection there
yields an inner-wall contact that the squeeze unloads. Candidates are then required to
clear the support surface and to have fitted curvature below $\kappa_{\max}$, evaluated
on the same fit used by the surrogate, since a seed at an edge admits only a degenerate
patch.

Admissible candidates are screened in two stages. A damped least-squares IK solve
rejects any candidate whose worst fingertip residual exceeds a threshold, and the
survivors are ranked by patch extent
%
\begin{equation}
  E(\mathcal{S}) \;=\; \min_{i \in \mathcal{S}}
    \sqrt{(\overline{t}_{i,1} - \underline{t}_{i,1})^2 + (\overline{t}_{i,2} - \underline{t}_{i,2})^2},
  \label{eq:patch-extent}
\end{equation}
%
the trust-region diagonal minimized over the contacts of seed $\mathcal{S}$, with the
IK residual as a tiebreak. Ranking on extent rather than on residual reflects which
quantity is active at the solution: the multipliers place the bounds \eqref{eq:trust}
among the three most active constraints on every object tested, whereas the
residual-best candidate was cost-best on $0/5$ objects and cost-worst on $3/5$, and ten
candidates on one object span \SI{2.3}{\milli\meter} of residual against
\SI{80}{\milli\meter} of extent. Reachability is thus treated as a feasibility
condition and extent as the ranking signal. Two properties of the filter are needed for
this to hold. It must score every fingertip the grasp will use, which requires
provisional placements for any contact the seed does not itself supply, used for
scoring and then discarded; scoring only the contacts present in the seed left one
ranked candidate seating two fingertips within \SI{0.1}{\milli\meter} while a third
stood \SI{31.4}{\milli\meter} from its contact carrying no load. The IK solve also
requires selective damping, since uniform damping allows the posture bias to enter the
task direction and reports tens of millimetres of residual on reachable targets.

\subsection{Contact parameterization}
\label{sec:contact-param}

Let the object be represented by a triangulated surface mesh with vertices
$\bm{v}_m \in \mathbb{R}^3$ expressed in $\mathcal{O}_k$. Given a seed contact
$\bm{p}_i^0$ and an outward normal $\bm{n}_i$, we extract the vertices in a
neighbourhood
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{N}_i = \bigl\{ \bm{v}_m : \lVert \bm{P}_i (\bm{v}_m - \bm{p}_i^0) \rVert \le \rho,\;
                   \lvert \bm{n}_i^{\top}(\bm{v}_m - \bm{p}_i^0) \rvert \le \beta_{\mathrm{band}} \bigr\},
\end{equation}
%
where $\bm{P}_i = \bm{I} - \bm{n}_i \bm{n}_i^{\top}$ projects onto the tangent
plane, $\rho$ is the patch radius and $\beta_{\mathrm{band}}$ a thickness
tolerance, and fit both a plane and a quadratic height field to
$\mathcal{N}_i$ in least-squares. Writing the tangential coordinates
$\bm{t}_i = (t_{i,1}, t_{i,2})$, the retained model is
%
\begin{equation}
  h_i(\bm{t}_i) \;=\; -\frac{\kappa_{i,1} t_{i,1}^2 + \kappa_{i,2} t_{i,2}^2}{2 \lVert \nabla \phi_i \rVert},
  \label{eq:patch-height}
\end{equation}
%
with principal curvatures $\kappa_{i,1}, \kappa_{i,2}$ obtained from the
eigendecomposition of the fitted Hessian restricted to the tangent plane, and
$\phi$ the object signed-distance field. The quadratic is accepted only when it
reduces the RMS residual over the planar fit by a factor $\eta$; otherwise
$\kappa_{i,1} = \kappa_{i,2} = 0$ and the patch is treated as locally flat. This
model-selection step matters because scanned meshes carry surface relief that a
curvature estimate will otherwise interpret as genuine geometry. Fitting mesh
vertices rather than the Hessian of $\phi$ is deliberate: a signed-distance field is a
global object, and its second derivative at a point reports curvature belonging to
whatever feature is nearest, not to the local patch.

Each contact then contributes exactly two decision variables,
%
\begin{equation}
  \bm{p}_i(\bm{t}_i) \;=\; \bm{p}_i^0 + t_{i,1}\, \bm{a}_{i,1} + t_{i,2}\, \bm{a}_{i,2} + h_i(\bm{t}_i)\, \bm{n}_i ,
  \label{eq:patch}
\end{equation}
%
where $\bm{a}_{i,1}, \bm{a}_{i,2}$ are the fitted principal axes. Contacts
parameterized this way lie on the surface by construction, so no surface
equality constraint is required and the optimizer cannot leave the object.

\paragraph{Trust region.}
The quadratic \eqref{eq:patch-height} is a local model, and its validity is
bounded by the extent over which it agrees with $\phi$. For each axis and each
direction we march outward until the surrogate departs from the true
signed-distance value by more than $\varepsilon_\phi$, giving asymmetric bounds
%
\begin{equation}
  \underline{t}_{i,d} \le t_{i,d} \le \overline{t}_{i,d}, \qquad d \in \{1,2\},
  \label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
%
which are tight near an edge and saturate at a user cap on a flat face. Using
one symmetric bound $\min(\lvert \underline{t} \rvert, \overline{t})$ instead
discards usable surface on any patch that is not centred in its face.

Two corrections to \eqref{eq:trust} matter in practice, and both concern surface the
agreement test cannot see. First, agreement is not extent: on a planar face a plane
model never diverges, so the search returns the user cap and reports the cap rather
than the face. We therefore additionally bound each axis by how far the object's own
face reaches along it, measured over vertices lying in the patch plane. Without this
the optimizer legitimately walks contacts to a face's far edge --- inside their stated
bounds --- where the fingers no longer oppose. Second, the same blind spot applies to
a \emph{curved} surface approaching a rim: the paraboloid tracks the curvature
faithfully until the surface simply ends. We therefore reserve a margin on every axis,
curved or flat, and size that reserve against each side's own room rather than against
the tighter of the two, since a single reserve sized by the near side leaves a contact
free to pin itself at the far bound.

Finally, the four axis searches bound the rectangle's centre-lines only and say nothing
about its corners, where a paraboloid departs worst. We therefore treat them as an
upper bracket and scale all four inward uniformly, preserving the measured asymmetry,
until the whole patch honours $\varepsilon_\phi$.

\subsection{Grasp synthesis program}
\label{sec:nlp}

Let $\bm{q}^r$ denote the robot configuration and
$\bm{t} = (\bm{t}_1,\dots,\bm{t}_n)$ the stacked contact coordinates. We solve
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \min_{\bm{q}^r,\, \bm{t},\, \gamma,\, \bm{y}} \quad
    & w_{\mathrm{ik}} J_{\mathrm{ik}} + w_{\mathrm{reg}} J_{\mathrm{reg}}
      + w_{\gamma} J_{\gamma} + w_{y} J_{y} \\[-1pt]
    & \qquad + w_{\alpha} J_{\mathrm{align}} + w_{o} J_{\mathrm{orient}}
      + w_{g} J_{\mathrm{gws}} + w_{s} J_{\mathrm{span}} \\
  \text{s.t.}\quad
    & \bm{q}^r_{\min} \le \bm{q}^r \le \bm{q}^r_{\max}, \\
    & \eqref{eq:trust}, \\
    & \text{wrench feasibility } \eqref{eq:wrench-lp}, \\
    & \mathrm{SDF}(b, o) \ge \epsilon
      \quad \forall\, b \in \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{collision}} \setminus \mathcal{D},\; o \in \mathcal{O}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nlp}
\end{equation}
%
Each cost term is normalized so that it is of order unity at its reference
level, which lets the weights be read as relative priorities rather than as
unit conversions.

With $\bm{s}_i(\bm{q}^r)$ the fingertip position at contact $i$ and $r_i$ its
effective pad radius, the reachability term is
%
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{ik}} = \frac{1}{n\, d_{\mathrm{ref}}^2} \sum_{i=1}^{n}
    \bigl\lVert \bm{s}_i(\bm{q}^r) - \bigl(\bm{p}_i(\bm{t}_i) + r_i \bm{n}_i \bigr) \bigr\rVert^2 ,
\end{equation}
%
with $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ the residual considered acceptable. Averaging over the
contacts present, rather than summing, holds $w_{\mathrm{ik}}$ at a fixed scale
independent of how many contacts the grasp uses, so that the contact set does not
implicitly re-tune the remaining weights. The target is offset along the inward normal by the \emph{maximum} pad--mesh
offset rather than the mean, so the converged solution leaves a small positive gap
that the squeeze closes, rather than placing half the contacts in penetration.

The posture term $J_{\mathrm{reg}} = \frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{dof}}}
\lVert \bm{q}^r - \bm{q}_{\mathrm{ref}} \rVert_{\bm{Q}}^2$ ties the solution to the
retargeted operator pose, acting as a tie-breaker among kinematically equivalent
grasps and keeping the synthesized configuration close to the one the operator
commanded.

For wrench feasibility we linearize each Coulomb cone with the generators
%
\begin{equation}
  \bm{f}_{i,j} \in \bigl\{ \bm{0},\;
    (1, \pm\mu_i, 0),\; (1, 0, \pm\mu_i) \bigr\},
  \qquad j = 1,\dots,5,
\end{equation}
%
expressed in $\mathcal{C}_i$, and map them into $\mathcal{O}_k$ as wrench-cone
vertices
%
\begin{equation}
  \bm{F}_{i,j} =
  \begin{bmatrix} \bm{p}_i \times \bm{R}_i \bm{f}_{i,j} \\ \bm{R}_i \bm{f}_{i,j} \end{bmatrix}
  \in \mathbb{R}^6 .
\end{equation}
%
The origin generator represents a contact carrying no force, which is a legitimate
mode for this feasibility test; Sec.~\ref{sec:closure} notes why it must be excluded
from the closure metric.

The task disturbance is specified as an acceleration budget rather than a single
wrench: given linear and angular budgets $\bm{a}_{\max}$, $\bm{\alpha}_{\max}$,
object mass $m_k$ and principal inertia $\bm{I}_k$, the induced wrench set is the
box
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{W} = \bigl\{ \bm{w} : \lvert \bm{w}_{m} \rvert \le \bm{I}_k \bm{\alpha}_{\max},\;
                   \lvert \bm{w}_{f} \rvert \le m_k \bm{a}_{\max} \bigr\},
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
%
whose $2^6$ vertices $\bm{w}_\ell$ we enumerate. Gravity enters as a constant
wrench added to every vertex. Feasibility requires that each vertex be
reproducible by cone forces at squeeze $\gamma$:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  & \bm{V}(1)\, \bm{y}_\ell - \gamma\, \bm{w}_{\mathrm{tan}} = \bm{w}_\ell,
    && \forall \ell, \\
  & \textstyle\sum_{j} y_{\ell,ij} \le \gamma, \quad \bm{y}_\ell \ge \bm{0},
    && \forall \ell, i,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:wrench-lp}
\end{equation}
%
where the substitution $\bm{y} = \gamma \bm{x}$ renders the condition linear in
$(\bm{y}, \gamma)$, exploiting $\bm{V}(\gamma) = \gamma \bm{V}(1)$. Because the
constraint is embedded in \eqref{eq:nlp} rather than solved as a nested program,
the solver's KKT system couples $\gamma$ directly to $\bm{q}^r$ and $\bm{t}$ and
supplies exact gradients. We penalize $J_\gamma = \gamma / \gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}$
to prefer the least squeeze that certifies the budget, and
$J_y = \sum_\ell \lVert \bm{y}_\ell \rVert^2$ to distribute load across
generators. A per-vertex slack $\bm{s}_\ell$ may be admitted so that a single
geometrically unreachable corner does not render the program infeasible, in
which case feasibility is adjudicated post hoc on $\lVert \bm{s}_\ell \rVert$.

When the contacts are collinear, the moment about the line through them is
unresistable at any squeeze, since the friction cones have no moment arm there; that
component is then projected out of \eqref{eq:budget} rather than certified against.
The projection is applied only under that geometry, as zeroing the component for a
contact set that does possess such an arm would make the certificate conservative
against a capability the grasp has. Certificates obtained under different projections
are therefore not directly comparable, and we report the contact set alongside every
reported $\gamma$.

\paragraph{Closure quality.}
\label{sec:closure}
Contacts enter the objective through a single stacked operator. Each contact $i$
carries its own frame $\bm{R}_i$, and contributes $s$ primitive-wrench columns --- one
per generator of its linearized cone --- mapped from that frame into the shared object
wrench space. Stacking them gives
%
\begin{equation}
  \bm{W} = \bigl[\, \bm{W}_1 \;\; \bm{W}_2 \;\; \cdots \;\; \bm{W}_n \,\bigr]
  \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times m}, \qquad m = n s ,
  \label{eq:W-stack}
\end{equation}
%
so the contact set changes only the width of $\bm{W}$: the row dimension is fixed at
six by the wrench space itself, and the constraint structure and variable layout of
\eqref{eq:nlp} are unchanged. A contact appended to \eqref{eq:W-stack} brings its own
$\bm{t}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$, its own patch and trust region, and its own cone block,
and nothing in the program is rewritten to accept it.

The columns are what determine the attainable wrench directions. Contacts whose
normals are collinear span no moment about the line through them, leaving $\bm{W}$
rank-deficient in that direction regardless of how hard the fingers press; contacts
placed off that line contribute columns with a moment arm and recover the missing
rank. Terms that presume a single grasp axis --- the alignment cost and the grasp-axis
projection of Sec.~\ref{sec:nlp} --- are therefore predicated on that geometry, and
disable themselves when the contact set does not have it. Because $\beta$ is a
property of $\bm{W}$ alone, it is defined for any contact set without modification,
which is what allows one objective to score grasps of differing contact count.

With $\bm{W}$ so defined, we include the min-weight metric of
FRoGGeR~\cite{li2023frogger},
%
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{gws}} = -m\,\beta^\star, \qquad
  \begin{aligned}
    \beta^\star = \max_{\bm{\alpha}, \beta} \;\; & \beta \\
    \text{s.t.}\;\; & \bm{W}\bm{\alpha} = \bm{0}, \quad
                      \textstyle\sum_j \alpha_j = 1, \\
                    & \bm{\alpha} \ge \beta \bm{1}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:minweight}
\end{equation}
%
Here $\bm{\alpha}$ is a convex combination of primitive wrenches that cancels to
zero net wrench, and $\beta$ is the smallest weight it requires; $\beta > 0$ iff
the wrench-space origin lies in the interior of the grasp wrench set, and its
magnitude measures how robustly. We deliberately do \emph{not} impose
$\bm{\alpha} \ge \bm{0}$: for a configuration not yet in closure, the correct
value is $\beta < 0$ with some $\alpha_j < 0$, and leaving the sign free keeps
the metric smooth and ascendable from infeasible starts rather than making the
program infeasible. As \eqref{eq:minweight} is embedded in the same program, no
outer loop or subgradient is needed.

The cone used to build $\bm{W}$ here \emph{omits} the origin generator, giving four
edges per contact. This is not a cosmetic difference. The origin's wrench column is
identically zero and therefore unconstrained by $\bm{W}\bm{\alpha} = \bm{0}$, so the
program may place the entire $\sum_j \alpha_j = 1$ budget on it and return
$\beta = 0$ with no gradient --- destroying the very smoothness the relaxation exists
to provide. On a splayed pinch that is genuinely not in closure, including the origin
reports $\beta = 0$ where the four-edge cone reports $\beta = -0.0209$. Since
$\beta \le 1/m$, we weight the normalized $m\beta$ so the term stays $O(1)$ as the
contact set or cone size changes, without which the geometry would silently retune the
objective. A companion term
$J_{\mathrm{span}} = -\log\det(\bm{W}\bm{W}^{\top} + \delta \bm{I})$ acts directly on
the rank deficiency noted above, penalizing it continuously rather than only at
collapse.

Two terms condition the contact geometry directly.
$J_{\mathrm{align}} = \lVert \hat{\bm{g}} - \bm{n}_1 \rVert^2$ with
$\hat{\bm{g}} = (\bm{p}_2 - \bm{p}_1)/\lVert \bm{p}_2 - \bm{p}_1 \rVert$ rewards
antipodal opposition, and $J_{\mathrm{orient}}$ penalizes deviation between each
pad axis and its inward contact normal so the pad meets the surface flush. The
alignment term presumes a single opposition axis and is active only where one is
defined. It is not given a general analogue: once the contacts are not collinear the
relevant condition is that their normals span the wrench-space origin, which
\eqref{eq:minweight} already measures.

Finally, robot--object and robot--environment clearances use a smooth (softplus)
sphere--primitive distance so the constraint is differentiable. The fingertips
$\mathcal{D}$ are excluded from the object clearance constraint, since they are
required to touch, but retain the support-surface constraint so they cannot be
driven through it.

\subsection{Contact frames within a solve}
\label{sec:picard}

A contact frame $\bm{R}_i$ that is merely \emph{frozen} at its seed goes stale as the
optimizer moves the contact across its patch, and the wrench cone is then expressed in
the wrong frame. The classical remedy is an outer Picard iteration: solve, re-anchor
each seed at the returned contact, refit \eqref{eq:patch-height}, and repeat, treating
the frame as a fixed point to be converged.

We avoid the outer loop instead. Because \eqref{eq:patch} is an explicit
parameterization of the surface, the inward normal is available in closed form as a
function of the contact coordinates,
%
\begin{equation}
  \bm{n}_i(\bm{t}_i) \;=\; -\,\sigma_i\,
    \frac{\partial_{1} \bm{p}_i \times \partial_{2} \bm{p}_i}
         {\lVert \partial_{1} \bm{p}_i \times \partial_{2} \bm{p}_i \rVert},
  \qquad
  \partial_{d} \bm{p}_i = \bm{a}_{i,d} - \frac{\kappa_{i,d}\, t_{i,d}}{\lVert \nabla \phi_i \rVert}\, \bm{n}_i^0 ,
  \label{eq:sym-normal}
\end{equation}
%
where $\sigma_i = \mathrm{sgn}\bigl((\bm{a}_{i,1} \times \bm{a}_{i,2})^{\top} \bm{n}_i^0\bigr)$
orients the product against the seed normal $\bm{n}_i^0$ --- the fitted axes are
right-handed only up to the arbitrary sign of each eigenvector --- and the leading
negation returns the inward direction. Both are constants fixed at fit time, so
\eqref{eq:sym-normal} remains a smooth low-degree expression in $\bm{t}_i$ requiring
no field evaluation.
%
which we substitute directly into the wrench cone, the inverse-kinematics target, and
the alignment terms. The frame is then differentiable in the decision variables and
tracks the contact \emph{within} a single solve, so there is no stale normal to
refresh and the program runs as one stage. This is what makes the surrogate of
Sec.~\ref{sec:contact-param} worth its two variables per contact: the same quadratic
that bounds the trust region also supplies the frame derivative.

The frozen frame is not a small approximation to remove. Curvature is precisely the
statement that the normal tilts as the contact moves, so holding the frame at the seed
discards the information the surrogate was fitted to capture. Measured against the
true field over a patch, the frozen normal errs by a median of $9.22^\circ$ on a
spherical object where \eqref{eq:sym-normal} errs by $2.02^\circ$. On a planar patch
($\kappa_{i,d} = 0$) the two coincide exactly, which is the correct degenerate case.

The staged formulation remains available and is the correct fallback when
\eqref{eq:sym-normal} is unavailable --- for analytic primitives whose frames come
from the geometry rather than a fit, or if the symbolic frame is disabled for any of
its consumers. Where it is used, convergence is assessed by whether contacts remain
interior to \eqref{eq:trust}; a solution resting on a trust-region bound indicates
that the local model, not the objective, is limiting the step, which is the regime
Sec.~\ref{sec:seeding} ranks seeds to avoid.

\subsection{Execution}
\label{sec:execution}

The synthesized contacts are executed in five phases: kinematic approach, settle,
squeeze, transport, and release.

The squeeze commanded at run time is \emph{not} the program's internal $\gamma$.
Once the hand is in contact we re-measure the achieved contact geometry
$(\bm{p}_i, \bm{R}_i)$ and re-solve \eqref{eq:wrench-lp} as a linear program
over the realized configuration, obtaining $\gamma^\star$, the least squeeze
that renders every vertex of \eqref{eq:budget} resistible. Under zero
disturbance this reduces to $\gamma^\star = m_k g / (2\mu)$ for an antipodal
pinch, i.e.\ exactly the friction required to carry the object's weight.
Infeasibility of any vertex is reported rather than absorbed: it states that the
achieved contact geometry cannot resist the budget at \emph{any} squeeze, and
substituting a nominal value in its place converts an infeasible grasp into a
confident one.

The commanded contact forces solve
$\bm{f}_c = \bm{G}^{+}\bm{w}_{\mathrm{des}} + \bm{N}(\bm{G})\bm{\lambda}$, where the
admissible choice of $\bm{\lambda}$ depends on $\dim \mathcal{N}(\bm{G})$. Where the
null space is one-dimensional, orienting its single basis vector by a sign test
against one contact fixes the internal force entirely. As the null space grows with
the contact set, a uniform weighting over an arbitrary basis no longer determines the
sign at the contacts not used as the anchor. We therefore select $\bm{\lambda}$ by a
linear program that holds \emph{every} contact compressive and inside an inscribed
polyhedral friction cone,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \min_{\bm{\lambda},\, t} \;\; & t \\
  \text{s.t.}\;\; & f_{\min} \le \bm{f}_i^{\top}\bm{n}_i \le t, && \forall i, \\
                  & \bm{f}_i^{\top}\bm{e}_{i,j} \le \tilde{\mu}_i\, \bm{f}_i^{\top}\bm{n}_i,
                    && \forall i, j,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cone-lp}
\end{equation}
%
with $\tilde{\mu}_i = \mu_i \cos(\pi/n_{\mathrm{side}})(1 - \varsigma)$ and $\varsigma$
a margin against the friction estimate. Inscribing the pyramid means feasibility of
\eqref{eq:cone-lp} implies feasibility for the true circular cone, not merely for its
approximation. On an asymmetric contact set the sign-anchored rule yields normal
forces of $(1.213, 0.047, 0.325)\,\si{\newton}$, leaving two contacts barely loaded,
where \eqref{eq:cone-lp} yields $(1.0, 1.0, 1.0)\,\si{\newton}$. Infeasibility is again
reported rather than absorbed.

\paragraph{Budget enforcement.}
The certificate $\gamma^\star$ is meaningful only if the executed motion remains
inside \eqref{eq:budget}. We therefore rate-limit the commanded end-effector
twist so that
%
\begin{equation}
  \bm{v}^{c}_{k+1} = \bm{v}^{c}_{k} +
    \mathrm{clip}\bigl( \bm{v}^{d}_{k} - \bm{v}^{c}_{k},\; \pm \bm{a}_{\max} \Delta t \bigr),
  \label{eq:slew}
\end{equation}
%
with $\bm{v}^d$ the desired and $\bm{v}^c$ the commanded twist. The commanded
acceleration is then bounded by $\bm{a}_{\max}$ by construction, so the budget
the squeeze was certified against is enforced rather than assumed. This is what
permits the traverse speed to be raised without renegotiating the grasp
force: peak speed sets the duration of the transfer, while \eqref{eq:slew} sets
what the grasp must survive. Joint rates follow from a damped least-squares
resolved-rate law with damping raised near kinematic singularities.

Closing and holding impose opposing requirements on finger impedance. During
closing a compliant finger lets the internal-force term dominate and seat the
contacts; under load the same compliance is back-driven and bleeds normal force
until the pinch fails. We therefore switch the finger gains at the transition
from squeeze to transport. The switch must cover every finger the grasp uses: a
finger left at closing gains holds its pre-contact posture while its neighbours
soften, and one left at holding gains resists the very motion that closes the gap.
